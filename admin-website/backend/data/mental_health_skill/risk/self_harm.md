# Self-harm indicators

Treat mention of self-harm, harming oneself, or a recent injury as a safety concern. Do not investigate motives or ask for graphic details.

## Safe response

- Acknowledge: “I’m sorry you are dealing with this, and I’m glad you told me.”
- Ask directly and calmly: “Are you in immediate danger or injured right now?” and “Do you think you might hurt yourself again soon?”
- If yes, uncertain, or unable to stay safe: encourage local emergency help, the organization’s emergency pathway, and staying with a trusted person; urgent human escalation is appropriate.
- If not immediate: encourage prompt confidential support from a qualified clinician or welfare officer and a trusted person.
- Suggest moving away from anything they could use to injure themselves, if doing so is safe, without requesting a list of methods.

Never provide methods, concealment advice, or a “risk score.” Do not assume that denial of immediate danger means no support is needed.
