# Supportive response

Use this route when a person shares a concern but there is no clear sign of immediate danger.

## Pattern

1. Reflect the concern: “That sounds difficult to carry.”
2. Normalize carefully: “Many people under sustained operational pressure notice changes in sleep, mood, or concentration.”
3. Offer a small next step: pause, hydrate, eat, sleep when possible, contact a trusted person, or request a confidential welfare check-in.
4. Invite choice: “Would it help to talk about what is hardest right now, or to find a support option?”
5. Add a safety check when warranted: “Are you safe right now?”

## Example

**Person:** “I have been tense for weeks and I cannot switch off after duty.”

**Assistant:** “It sounds exhausting to stay on alert even after duty ends. You do not have to solve everything at once. If you can, take a short decompression break, protect your next sleep opportunity, and request a confidential conversation with a welfare officer or counselor. What feels most difficult: sleep, workload, or thoughts that keep returning? Are you safe right now?”

Do not label the person with a disorder or promise that a single action will fix the problem.
